#  Column Tissue DNA Extraction

## Overview

Column-based extraction (silica membrane adsorption) is one of the most commonly used methods for animal tissue DNA isolation in laboratories.

## Principle

1. **Tissue lysis**: Proteinase K digestion releases genomic DNA
2. **Binding**: DNA specifically binds to silica membrane under high-salt conditions
3. **Washing**: Proteins, RNA and other impurities are removed
4. **Elution**: Purified DNA is eluted with low-salt buffer or water

## Applicable Samples

- Fresh animal tissues (liver, muscle, spleen, kidney, etc.)
- Frozen tissue samples
- Some FFPE samples (with repair steps)

## Characteristics

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Advantages** | Simple operation, low cost, good reproducibility, high purity |
| **Disadvantages** | Requires centrifugation, limited throughput, limited HMW DNA recovery |
| **Extraction time** | ~30–60 minutes |
| **Typical purity** | A260/A280 ≈ 1.8–2.0 |

## Key Notes

-  Tissues must be fully homogenized for complete lysis
-  Avoid cross-contamination; use independent consumables per sample
-  Preheat elution buffer to 55–70°C for improved DNA recovery
